## [Physicists Solve Muon Mystery, Make Old Results Inconsistent](https://www.quantamagazine.org/physicists-solve-a-muon-mystery-now-old-results-dont-add-up-20260729/) ⭐️ 9.0/10

Physicists have solved the long-standing muon mystery, and the new understanding makes previously published experimental results no longer add up. The resolution, reported by Quanta Magazine, challenges the earlier interpretation of muon anomaly measurements. This breakthrough is significant because it forces a reassessment of decades of muon g-2 data and their implications for the Standard Model. Correcting the interpretation may determine whether the anomaly points to new physics or stems from subtle errors in older experiments. The muon anomaly is measured as aμ = (g−2)/2, and Fermilab's Muon g-2 experiment achieved a final precision of 127 parts per billion, making it one of the most stringent tests of the Standard Model. Because a discrepancy had persisted since Brookhaven measurements in the late 1990s, the new solution implies that earlier datasets must be reanalyzed.

hackernews · ibobev · Jul 30, 15:22 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49111305)

**Background**: The muon is a heavier cousin of the electron, and its magnetic moment receives small quantum corrections from virtual particles; these corrections are described by the "anomalous magnetic moment." The Muon g-2 experiment at Fermilab was designed to measure this quantity with extremely high precision and compare it to Standard Model predictions. A significant mismatch, known as the muon g-2 anomaly, had been interpreted by many as a hint of undiscovered particles. The newly solved mystery apparently resolves this mismatch in a way that invalidates older results.

## [Kimi K3: How Moonshot Engineered Its Way to the Frontier](https://www.reddit.com/r/MachineLearning/comments/1vaysjf/how_kimi_k3_engineered_its_way_to_the_frontier_r/) ⭐️ 9.0/10

Moonshot AI's Kimi K3, an open-weight model ranking 4th among 580 models on Artificial Analysis, is analyzed in depth for its three engineering breakthroughs: Kimi Delta Attention, Quantile Balancing, and the AgentENV runtime. The 47-page technical report and released code reveal how each innovation contributes to frontier-level performance. This demonstrates that open-weight models can compete with the best proprietary systems through novel engineering rather than sheer scale. K3's innovations in attention, expert load balancing, and RL training infrastructure could influence future model designs across the industry. Kimi Delta Attention replaces the KV cache in 69 of 93 layers with a single 128x128 matrix per head, shrinking a 1M-token context from 104.6 GiB to 27.2 GiB. Quantile Balancing keeps 896 experts per layer evenly loaded by computing bias directly from one batch's router score margins, while AgentENV created 51 million Firecracker microVM sandboxes with 133 ms checkpoints and 49 ms resumes for RL training.

reddit · r/MachineLearning · /u/noninertialframe96 · Jul 30, 16:37

**Background**: Transformer LLMs rely on a KV cache to store past keys and values for efficient generation, which grows with context length; Kimi Delta Attention (KDA) is a linear attention mechanism extending Gated DeltaNet with finer-grained decay to compress this state. Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) models use many specialized experts per layer and need load balancing to avoid underutilization; Quantile Balancing is a hyperparameter-free method introduced by Jianlin Su. AgentENV is an open-source platform powering agentic RL training for Kimi K3, using Firecracker microVMs to run sandboxed agent environments at scale.

## [The session you cannot take with you: AI's subtle lock-in](https://earendil.com/posts/session-portability/) ⭐️ 8.0/10

A new essay argues that AI conversations are not portable across models or providers: the transcript on your machine is only a partial view, while the session's operational state belongs to the inference provider. This creates a subtle but real ecosystem lock-in that users should actively resist by switching. As AI assistants handle increasingly important work, session portability directly affects user freedom and market competition. If providers own session state, switching costs rise, making it harder for users to choose the best model and easier for vendors to entrench their ecosystems. The article clarifies that portability does not mean switching models must produce identical next tokens; it means users should own the full session's operational state. It also notes that provider moats come from bundling extensions like web search and code execution as simple tools, even though these are in theory separable from the inference API.

hackernews · apitman · Jul 31, 03:47 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49118781)

**Background**: AI conversation history is usually stored by the provider, and data portability formats exist for exporting chat logs, but true session portability is harder because it includes operational state such as tool calls, memory, and context. Many providers bundle non-LLM extensions that are conceptually separate but tightly coupled in practice, increasing switching costs. The article appears in the context of a broader debate about AI platform lock-in, where enterprises and developers worry about fragmentation and closed ecosystems.

The article explains the technical and practical reasons behind the push for solid-state batteries, including higher energy density and improved safety. The accompanying Hacker News discussion adds detailed context on different electrolyte types, military drone uses, and the limitations of the 'solid-state' analogy. Solid-state batteries could replace conventional lithium-ion cells in electric vehicles, consumer electronics, and drones, where weight and safety are critical. A breakthrough would have a major impact on the energy-storage industry and on any battery-dependent technology. Not all solid-state batteries prevent dendrite growth, as commenters note; one promising flavor is a polymer single-ion conductor with low activation energy and no phase transitions between -40°C and 80°C. Theoretically, this enables higher energy density and improved safety because flammable liquid solvents are replaced by non-flammable solids. One key challenge is lithium dendrite growth, which can short-circuit cells even in solid-electrolyte batteries.

## [MLVC: Learned Video Codec Designed for Real-World Cross-Platform Deployment](https://www.reddit.com/r/MachineLearning/comments/1vb3xwd/mlvc_multiplatform_learned_video_codec_for/) ⭐️ 8.0/10

The authors introduce MLVC, a multi-platform neural video codec that addresses cross-platform compatibility by transmitting entropy-model scale parameters via the hyperprior, avoiding the need for bit-exact execution across NPUs. It runs at around 100 FPS for 360p/540p video on consumer NPUs and has been open-sourced by Microsoft. MLVC is significant because cross-platform incompatibility has been a key barrier preventing learned video codecs from replacing traditional codecs like H.264, H.265, and AV1. By combining real-time performance, competitive compression, and hardware robustness, it moves neural video codecs closer to actual deployment on diverse consumer devices. MLVC is based on the DCVC family of neural video codecs open-sourced by Microsoft Research, and it reportedly achieves over 70% MOS-based BD-rate improvement over hardware HEVC. The design explicitly transmits entropy-model scale parameters through the hyperprior so encoding and decoding need not be bit-exact, because current NPU hardware and toolchains lack standardized fixed-point math.

reddit · r/MachineLearning · /u/tanelai · Jul 30, 19:40

**Background**: Traditional video codecs such as H.264, H.265, and AV1 dominate real-world use partly because they have widespread hardware acceleration and low power consumption, while neural codecs are larger and more power-hungry. A further obstacle is that neural codecs rely on entropy models, and small numerical differences between NPUs can cause encoder-decoder disagreement that breaks decoding. Quantizing models or switching to integer math does not reliably fix this because hardware features like rounding modes, accumulation data types, and scale multiplication are not fully standardized.
