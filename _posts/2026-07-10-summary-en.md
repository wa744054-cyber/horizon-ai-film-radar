## [Why American ambulance rides are so expensive](https://davidoks.blog/p/why-american-ambulance-rides-are) ⭐️ 8.0/10

The article examines structural factors behind high US ambulance costs, arguing that low Medicare and insurance reimbursements force providers to charge higher rates to privately insured patients, rather than simply blaming greed. This analysis matters because it reveals systemic pricing failures in US healthcare, potentially influencing policy discussions on surprise billing and ambulance fee schedules. The article draws an options analogy to explain ambulance economics, though some commentators find it forced. Balance billing remains a key problem, with patients often receiving surprise bills.

hackernews · jyunwai · Jul 9, 22:15 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48853091)

**Background**: In the US, ambulance services are provided by a mix of public and private entities. The Emergency Medical Treatment and Labor Act (EMTALA) requires hospitals to treat all emergency patients, but does not fully cover ambulance transport. Medicare reimburses via an Ambulance Fee Schedule, often below actual costs, prompting providers to charge higher rates to privately insured patients through balance billing.

## [Best Practices for TLS Certificates in Internal Services](https://tuxnet.dev/posts/tls-for-internal-services/) ⭐️ 8.0/10

An article and community discussion advocate for using ACME DNS validation instead of split-horizon DNS for managing TLS certificates in internal services. This shift simplifies certificate management, reduces operational complexity, and avoids the pitfalls of split-horizon DNS such as inconsistent behavior and debugging challenges. Split-horizon DNS can lead to different DNS responses for internal vs. external clients, causing silent failures. ACME DNS validation uses DNS TXT records to prove domain control, allowing certificates without public HTTP servers.

hackernews · mrl5 · Jul 9, 14:57 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48846995)

**Background**: Split-horizon DNS provides different DNS information based on the query source, often used to separate internal and external access. ACME DNS validation is a standard method for certificate authorities to verify domain ownership by requiring a specific DNS TXT record. Many internal services struggle with certificate management due to split DNS complexities.
