The essay argues that truly good tools become invisible by minimizing interface friction and cognitive overhead, allowing users to focus on their tasks rather than the tool itself. This perspective challenges design trends that add unnecessary complexity, urging software creators to prioritize simplicity and flow. It resonates with developers and designers aiming for productivity and user satisfaction. The essay uses the metaphor of invisibility to describe tools that don't demand attention—like a well-designed kitchen knife. US export controls on AI have not broadly prohibited Chinese parent companies from accessing advanced models via non-Chinese subsidiaries, creating the loophole exploited here.

Globally, it is the second-largest IPO ever after SpaceX's $85.7 billion raise, and the largest ever by a foreign company in the United States.
